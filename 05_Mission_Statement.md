## 1. The mission

> **We map what AI makes possible, not what AI does.**
>
> - **The object.** Businesses, markets and institutions that become viable because AI, or the build-out behind it, collapses a general-purpose cost. Their customers buy something that is not AI: a home, a spare part, a meal, a clinic visit, a title deed, an insurance policy, a service from another country.
> - **The ecosystem, not the first user.** The first firm to use the cheap input is the railway. We are after the horse trade at the station: the complements, intermediaries, finance, standards, insurers, public offices, places and uses of freed time that exist only once thousands of those firms do.
> - **The timing.** We are asking a deployment-period question during an installation period. Cognition is getting cheaper fast: about 75× a year for a capability that has just reached the frontier, and about 4.7× a year two years later. The physical inputs the build-out consumes are getting dearer for everyone else. Ecosystems that need only a digital complement can form now. Those waiting on a plant, a statute, a standard or a critical mass wait for that complement, not for the calendar.
> - **The method.** For each chain we name the cost that falls, the threshold at which the new output pays, the second unlock it waits for, the ecosystem that follows, who captures the rent and who funds it, and the dated signals that would confirm or kill it.
> - **The blocker.** What binds now is the most important thing we collect. For every chain we name the blocker that keeps the marginal entrant or buyer out. We quantify where it blocks, why, and by how much; we classify it; and we measure how far AI is from removing it. A blocker class removed in one industry points to every industry where the same class binds. That is how we generalise and extrapolate.
> - **The signal.** We read every chain the way the third derivative is defined: the acceleration of acceleration. We track it on each chain's binding cost and on the count of its complements, so the inflection shows up here before it is public.
> - **The reverse.** We map what the build-out makes dearer, and whom it un-enables.
> - **The discipline.** We commit to probabilities and dates before the evidence arrives, and we are scored on them. Evidence tests the mechanism and sets the odds. It never decides which ideas we are allowed to hold.

**The one-line test:** if the customer is buying the AI, or an existing firm is using AI to do its existing job better, it is out. Everything else is in. §4 makes this operational as three gates.

## 2. The question, and the two questions we keep apart

- **Our question (the project owner, 15 Oct 2026):** which businesses become viable "thanks to AI because AI brings some kind of overall cost down enough to allow for other services to become cheap enough for broader adaption and creation of completely new eco systems", looking "one step further".
- **The worked exemplar is fibre → Netflix and YouTube.**
  - US transit fell from about $1,200 per Mbps in 1998 to about $25 per Mbps in 2007. Norton calls the series an anecdotal midpoint, not one market price (V1, claim 12, PARTLY). Map 03 records the same path as about $1,200, then about $200, then about $25 per Mbps-month (1998, 2002, 2007; 03 §2). V1 notes that his historical table does not print "per month".
  - Netflix introduced "Watch Now" on 16 Jan 2007, rolling it out through June (V1, claim 12, PARTLY; the Netflix press release was not retrieved). Map 03 dates Netflix streaming to January 2007.
  - The subscriber buys films, not fibre.
  - One step further is the creator economy. It was not sized (03 §2).
- **The canal story is a shape, not a proof.** Canal → cheap coal → steam → rail is the right *shape* of question. The record corrects the history. The Bridgewater aqueduct opened in 1761. Manchester coal was about half price soon after: by 1762 on one account, or overnight when the canal reached central Manchester in 1763 on another. Which year is UNCERTAIN (V1, claim 11, PARTLY). That was about 30 years before the Canal Mania, 44 new-canal Acts in 1791–95 (01 §2, row H01; the 1761 date is 01 §3, correction 1; V1). Only a few of those canals made money for investors. Some were profitable. A number never paid a dividend or were never finished (V1, claim 11). Do not import the railway fact that about half the authorised mileage was never built (01 §3, correction 5). The lesson is that the *cheap input* did the work, not the *bubble*.
- **Not our question: the strict third claim.** That is a business living on capacity written down below its build cost. 01 §6 keeps it as an annex. The switch is **J3**. A filed change of control of energised, interconnected data-centre capacity at one-third or less of undepreciated cost, to a buyer that is not a hyperscaler, a neocloud, or an infrastructure fund. **Lead trigger:** the H100 transaction index's 30-day median falls below $2.60 and a GPU-backed loan breaches a covenant or a debt-service ratio (01 §6.4). On 14 Oct 2026, J3 was NOT FIRING. Precursors only. "Nothing survives the strict test" is not an answer to our question.
- **Not our question: direct use.** Firms using AI to do their own job better are excluded on sight (§4).

## 3. The nature of future ecosystems: what we expect them to look like

### 3.1 Where we are in the cycle

- **Perez's model runs in three stages** (Perez 2002 and 2010; V1, claim 1, PARTLY). Installation: infrastructure is built ahead of use, and its late phase is a frenzy and a financial bubble that collapses. Turning point: the interval after the bust, usually a serious recession. The turning point is not itself the crash. Deployment: the paradigm spreads. A golden age in early deployment can follow, depending on institutional choices. 01 §3 did not separately test this paragraph. The claims stay, with that limit.
- **October 2026 is early installation.** There is no bust. J3 is not firing (01 §6.4). The build-out is still bidding shared inputs away from others (03 §5).
- **Enabled ecosystems do not share one lag.** Aggregate productivity can lag for decades while a niche is already a trade. Newer technologies have been adopted faster than older ones. Where the complement is a digital good at sellable quality, and the reorganization fits inside one firm, the ecosystem can form during installation. Where the complement is a plant, a statute, a standard that needs a critical mass, or an information structure that does not wear out with time, the chain waits. Calendar time does not create the redesign. A mission that demands revenue in October 2026 finds the first niches. A mission that waits for a golden age finds the inflection after the public has seen it.
- **The productivity lags stay, and they are not the fibre lag.** Aggregate productivity lagged Watt's steam engine by about 80 years. Electricity's major boost to American growth came about 40 years after the first commercial generating stations (Crafts, May 2003; V1, claim 2, CONFIRMED). David dates the same electricity lag as four decades after the first central station (AER, May 1990). David also warns against importing that lag literally: information is not like electric current (same paper). Comin and Hobijn find that countries adopted technologies 45 years after invention on average, and that newer technologies were adopted faster (AER, December 2010). Each Perez period is on the order of three decades, and diffusion runs through feedback loops, not after a golden age is declared (Perez 2002).
- **Fibre is a different clock.** The measured fibre lag is 1–5 years, not 5–9 (03 §10, E01). YouTube about 3 years, Netflix about 5 (03 §1). AWS and M-PESA needed no glut (03 §2). A 2023–26 cognition crossing may show a flagship through about 2024–31 (03 §1, CALC). Do not group fibre with the 80-year or the 40-year productivity lag.
- **Route-dated kill windows.** These are the maximum "not yet" may last. They are not a new lag screen. **R1:** about 0–5 years (01 §4 L11). **R3:** about 1–5 years after the crossing (03 §1). **R4:** the statute or programme date. **R5 and physical chains:** only after J3. For a chain waiting on a plant, a statute, a standard or a critical mass, the kill date is the date the named complement was forecast to arrive and did not. **R2** has no fixed kill window. It is "not yet" only inside a named, dated complement. Historic R2 speed was 5–25 years (01 §4 L1; L11 gives 7–25 years for R2 and R5), and neither range is a screen. **Every R5 and physical row also carries a review date,** so its "not yet" ends even if J3 never fires.

**Assumption.** Reorganization and critical mass set the ecosystem date. The steam productivity lag does not. **Verdict:** SURVIVES.

### 3.2 Two clocks

| Clock | Input | Speed | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cognition** (route R3) | the price of a fixed level of AI capability: translation, drafting, design iterations, document reading, coordination, perception | **Three Epoch readings** (22 Sep 2026; rough measurements, not exact). Since 2023, about 47% a quarter, or about 13× a year. A level that has just debuted as state of the art: about 66% a quarter, or about 75× a year. Two years later: about 32% a quarter, or about 4.7× a year. The vintage pattern, the fast fall then the slower one, shows on three of five main benchmarks. On two of five the decline does not slow (V1, claim 4) | **cheap and getting cheaper, for everyone at once.** Steady rather than accelerating at the average rate. Flagship list prices are rising (01 §6.0, §6.1) |
| **Physical** (crowding, S7; later perhaps R1 or a glut) | power, grid, transformers, DRAM, fibre, turbines, electricians, sites | slow; set by factories and permits. *IEA, Energy and AI* (April 2025; the fetched page does not print the date): new transmission takes 4–8 years in advanced economies, and waits for transformers and cables doubled in three years | **the inputs the build-out is buying are getting dearer for everyone else.** Not every physical price is rising. See below |

Section 1 states the direction for the inputs the build-out consumes. It is not a claim that every physical price is rising.

**What is getting dearer:** DRAM, transformers, fibre, gas turbines and electricians (03 §5). **What is not:** SiC and lidar are down. LFP storage is mixed. Those curves were paid for by EVs, shale and ADAS (03 §5). **A DRAM glut is restoration.** The 2019 and 2023 memory gluts restored existing products. They did not found new ones (01 §4 L15; 03 §6). **Apart from by-product heat, physical enablement needs an AI-anchored decline that reaches others, or a bust (J3)** (01 §4 L15). InP optics is the plausible later anchor (03 §5).

**The physical clock is geographic.** Data centres are about a tenth of global electricity-demand growth to 2030, and nearly half of US growth (IEA, *Energy and AI*, as above). Nearly half of US data-centre capacity sits in five regional clusters (same source). About 20% of planned data-centre projects are exposed to delay if the grid risks are not fixed (same source). Ireland is the local illustration, not the world average: data centres were 21% of electricity consumption in 2023, and could be 32% in 2026 (Bruegel, 20 Feb 2025). Moving the data can be cheaper than moving the power (same article).

**Posted prices are two endpoints, not a curve.** Google's machine-translation list price was $20 per million characters in March 2014 and again in October 2026 (03 §3). Sellable quality is far inside the raw collapse. Post-edited translation costs about 90% less than human. Raw machine output is about 99.9% less (03 §3; China Literature, VENDOR, for the sellable figure). Every threshold is priced at sellable quality. Publishers' realised audiobook savings were 20% in year 2 and 50% in year 3, against vendor promises of 85–95% (Frankfurt Book Fair/Dosdoce, 85 experts; PRESS: PublishNews, 9 Sep 2026; 03 §3).

### 3.2a The third derivative

The public sees the level and the first difference. Cognition is cheap and still getting cheaper. The early signal is the next difference.

**Computation.** Work on trailing four-quarter log changes. Do not take raw third differences of quarterly prices. The third difference of a noisy quarterly series is mostly noise. For each chain, record the trailing four-quarter log change in the sellable-quality price of the binding constraint, the change in that change, and the change in the change. A signal needs two consecutive readings in the same direction. A confirmation is that pair of readings, a turn in the second difference, while revenue is still absent.

Keep the same three differences for two counts: specialist intermediaries, and standards or insurance forms that name the chain. The complement-count series stays a design until four quarters of data exist.

**Two families, two labels.** The dashboard keeps "annex J" (J1–J6, built for the strict third claim, 01 §5) apart from the "chain derivative" here. A J verdict is never copied onto a chain. Each live chain names its own sellable-quality price series. The scraped Epoch series is a clock, not a chain's price.

A capability that has just become state of the art cheapens at about 66% a quarter (about 75× a year). Two years later, at about 32% a quarter (about 4.7× a year). Epoch, 22 Sep 2026, on three of five main benchmarks. A chain that needs today's frontier is on the fast clock. A chain a two-year-old capability can already perform is on the slow one. Benchmark cost is not sellable-quality cost. Doing well on a benchmark is not the same as useful work (Epoch, same page).

**Assumption.** A turn in the acceleration of the binding constraint, and in complement formation, leads revenue. **Verdict:** SURVIVES WITH CHANGES. The vintage split is measured. The complement-count series is a design until the first four quarters exist.

### 3.3 Conjunctions: AI rarely enables alone

Across the ten K chains, AI cuts a real cost, and usually not the binding one. The binding constraint is a second factor. Figures from the K parcels of 16 Oct 2026 (`_grok_returns/K02`, `K04`, `K06`, `K07`, `K08_answer.md`).

| Chain | What binds |
|---|---|
| Infill housing (K02) | permit soft costs are only ~4% of a backyard project; fees, utilities and statute bind |
| Clinics (K07) | AI saves ~$2k a year on notes against ~$100k of billing cost |
| Micro-brands (K04) | the AI shoe start-up also relies on a printer that abolishes the mould |
| Land titles (K08) | title law and foreclosure rules bind |
| Unstaffed shops (K06) | the unlock was locks, ID checks and self-scan |

**Uber is not the example.** Uber fails this test (03 §2). The measured gain was Uber's own matching, not a fall in a general input. Geocaching, from 3 May 2000, after Selective Availability was switched off, is the template for a free capability crossing a quality line (03 §2). Netflix needed last-mile broadband and licences (03 §2).

**So the unit of analysis is the conjunction.** The AI cost fall comes together with a second unlock:

- a statute or permitting reform;
- a fabrication technology (additive manufacturing, robotics);
- a payment or identity rail;
- a liability or consent regime;
- a price for some other input;
- the cost of reorganizing the work, which can exceed the AI saving, and which does not arrive because the price fell (NBER w7136, 1999, QJE February 2002; NBER w25148, October 2018, journal 2021);
- a critical mass of the complement, because neither side of a feedback loop is viable alone (Perez 2002: without roads and stations the car is unusable, and without enough cars the station does not pay);
- a standard or a certificate that lets strangers trade.

**Attention and screening** are a cost, and a business. Negligible costs of distribution encourage indiscriminate broadcasting, and screening is costly (David, AER, May 1990). Curation, provenance and certification are that business. They are not only a sentence about a flooded tail.

A chain that is price-ready and density-short stays MECHANISM. The second unlock is usually the trigger to watch, and it often comes from outside AI.

**Assumption.** A cleared cost, without reorganization, density, or a standard, does not make the ecosystem. **Verdict:** SURVIVES.

### 3.4 The anatomy of an enabled ecosystem: nine layers

Every chain is mapped across nine layers. The list is a checklist to fill, not a finding that the layers have already formed:

1. the cheap input;
2. the first enabled users;
3. **complements** that stay scarce (licences, sites, distribution, records, brands, relationships);
4. intermediaries and platforms;
5. finance;
6. institutions and regulation;
7. skills;
8. the physical substrate;
9. **second-order trades**.

The non-obvious winner usually sits in layers 3, 4, 5 or 9. The historic tell is the horse at the station. Perez (2002) says horse numbers rose for more than 50 years because horse transport was still needed from railway stations to ships, houses and inns. Britain's horse stock grew from just over 1 million in 1811 to more than 3 million in 1901 (National Army Museum, undated page; V1, claim 3, PARTLY). The series starts in 1811, before the first inter-city railway (Liverpool and Manchester, 1830), so part of the rise is not railway-driven. UNCERTAIN that the rise was only, or mainly, haulage from stations (V1). The search is for the AI-era horse at the station: the trade that exists only once thousands of first users do.

**Where trust, liability, or homogeneity is the scarce complement, layer 6 is a market.** Certification and provenance. Audit. Professional indemnity and product warranty. The rating and servicing of pooled small tickets. These are **in** when what is insured or certified is a non-AI good or service: the part, the house, the visit. The underlying customer buys the house, the meal, the visit, or the part. The customer of this layer buys a certificate, a policy, or a serviced claim.

**Cover for AI systems themselves is out of the delivered book.** Model-deployer liability belongs to the AI supply chain. It goes to the second book: kept and scored, not delivered (§9).

Provenance standards exist. C2PA is opt-in, and a content credential records tamper-evidence rather than truth (C2PA specification 2.4 explainer; no date on the page). An opt-in standard that is not adopted is a failed complement. Standards, supervisory bodies and financial innovations were part of deployment, and installment credit was as necessary as the traffic code (Perez 2002).

**Assumption.** Verification and liability transfer become purchased markets once generation is cheap, and they pass the one-line test when the thing insured or certified is not AI. **Verdict:** SURVIVES WITH CHANGES. The standard exists and is opt-in. Insurance capacity was not measured. UNCERTAIN how large that market is in October 2026.

### 3.5 The shapes AI enablement will take

| Shape | What happens | Expected form |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | A fixed cost per unit of variety falls: per title, language, design, product, site, market | first **longer tails of existing categories**, and more, smaller firms, with more experiments and more failures (the AWS shape); new categories only one step further |
| **S2** | A metered price falls until heavy use fits a mass budget | heavy use is still above a mass budget. Cloud video understanding is about $26 (older tier, input only) to $65–86 per camera-month, against a about $5 threshold, and it is DIRECT anyway (03 §2). No passing instance yet |
| **S3** | A per-transaction cost falls until small tickets pay | so far blocked by credit and regulation. The institution to name is a pool, a servicer, a rating, a true-sale opinion, or a residual-value or warranty line. No observed pooling market yet. Credit scoring that can deny a loan is high-risk from 2 Dec 2027 |
| **S4** | Language and distance collapse | **a fork, not one shape.** See below |
| **S5** | The cost of trust changes | ambiguous: **deepfakes make trusted checks dearer.** The business is the certificate, the policy, or the serviced claim (§3.4) |
| **S6** | An anchor buyer pays for a learning curve that others inherit | the anchor buyer, often public procurement. Only 10 of 36 OECD countries (28%) report any impact measurement of AI use in government (OECD, 15 Jun 2026). A named private party made viable by a measured pilot: no instance yet |
| **S7** | The anchor buyer crowds out others | crowding and un-enablement. A first-class output, not a footnote. The dominant physical reading now (03 §2, §5) |
| **S8** | A free capability crosses a quality line | platform-supplied, so revocable. A quality step is not a cost collapse. eBay 2014, +10.9% on a layer that was already free, is a completed pre-build-out case (03 §1, §3). Predates stays out of the build-out result |
| **S9** | A by-product is priced at disposal value | waste heat. See below. Status: watch, weak |
| **S10** | A physical surplus is usable locally, before any bust | locational surplus, not "wait for the collapse". See below |
| **S11** | A statute or a public rail looks like a cost fall | a screen, not an AI result (03 §2; 01 §4 L16). Historic R4 happened. It is not discarded. It is not called AI |
| **S12** | Cognitive chores take fewer hours, and the hours are an input | a forecast with a probability, tied to a time-use series. A single visible pastime is forbidden. See below |
| **S13** | People are bought back for tasks they still do better | a human premium: presence, liability, care, judgment or taste. MECHANISM. See below |

**S4, in full.** Fork A: language and distance fall, and services are exported from places shut out of the buyer's labour market. Fork B: the same fall removes the manufacturing ladder developing economies used, and winner-take-most dynamics accrue to the countries that hold the models (NBER w28453, 2021). On a standard gravity structure there is no tipping point, and remote service work does not transform development. A different shape of latent advantage can turn a small cost fall into a large export of tasks (NBER w29387, 2021). Register both, with probabilities that may sum to less than one. Outside China, emerging economies hold about half of internet users and under a tenth of data-centre capacity (IEA, *Energy and AI*, April 2025; the fetched page does not print the date). The services clock and the power clock are not the same clock. **Assumption:** the two forks are genuine rivals. **Verdict:** SURVIVES.

**S9, waste heat.** Waste heat is the only disposal-priced AI flow (01 §6.1). It mostly predates the build-out. It is partly R4, where a statute pushes reuse. It is not free where a district network bids: about 400 SEK/MWh in Stockholm, February 2023, CALC (03 §2). No new-output food host was shown operating on AI heat as of 15 Oct 2026 (03, C-9). Status: watch, weak.

**S10, locational surplus.** A physical surplus can be local and prior to any bust. A node where renewable output is curtailed, or where grid waits push new load away from the cluster. The enabled party is the non-AI user of that surplus. The customer buys heat, water, materials, or food, not compute. Data centres are the rival bidder for the same node. The chain dies if that queue takes the slack before the non-AI load is connected. A system-wide sale of capacity below build cost stays the strict annex, switched on by J3 (§2). **The power-overbuild chain states its route, the substrate that would transfer, the demand the leftover network would still have, and why it is not H01's wrong network or H09's securities-only collapse** (01 §2, §4 L7–L8). The analogue is not self-certifying. **Assumption:** locational slack is usable by a non-AI load before a system-wide glut. **Verdict:** SURVIVES WITH CHANGES. Slack and the siting incentive are evidenced. Who wins the node is the forecast.

**S12, time.** When cognitive chores take fewer hours, the hours are an input. The chain names where a measured block of hours goes, the non-AI purchase it funds, and the date. In a six-month experiment, the 80% of treated workers who used the tool spent two fewer hours on email each week, in the second half, and the task mix did not move (NBER w33795, 2025). Until a time-use reading shows a destination, S12 stays MECHANISM with a pre-registered probability. A single visible pastime is forbidden as the map. **Assumption:** a time-use series can lead a non-AI spend, and the destination is not yet known. **Verdict:** SURVIVES WITH CHANGES. The stronger claim, that freed hours already imply a new paid ecosystem, FAILS on that experiment. Destination UNCERTAIN.

**S13, reinstated human tasks.** The purchase is a human premium, such as presence, liability, care, judgment or taste, in tasks where people keep the comparative advantage (Acemoglu and Restrepo, NBER w25684, March 2019; Journal of Economic Perspectives 2019). It is MECHANISM. It passes the gates only where the purchase is a new output, not the old job done by fewer people.

### 3.6 What is different this time

1. **The size map is a barbell, and geography splits with it.** Cheap cognition pulls the size distribution in two directions at once. At entry, teams that embed the capability in what they sell are smaller, flatter, and more expert-dense, and today's startup evidence clusters them where the models and the talent already are. Within industry and cohort, AI-native startups are 25% smaller. In services that used to scale by hiring, they are roughly 70% smaller, and nearly a full level flatter. Those teams concentrate in Silicon Valley, and their workforces are more male, more likely to hold advanced degrees, and drawn from more prestigious employers (HBS Working Paper 26-090, 9 Jun 2026). At the revenue end, past waves shifted sales toward a few high-markup, low-labour-share firms (NBER w23396, 2017; QJE 2020). The map is a barbell: many thin producers, each holding a complement imitation cannot copy, and a few holders of distribution, compute, or the standard. Tradable cognitive tasks can be performed far from the buyer. Sites, licences, grid nodes and relationships stay local. Installation also polarizes regions, new spaces against the old strongholds (Perez 2002). A services-export chain and a concentrated-platform chain are both in scope. They are rival forecasts until one of them takes the rent. **Caveat:** the small-team evidence is on AI-native firms, which §4 deletes. The extension to non-AI producers, and the repeat of superstar concentration, are forecasts. Applying the superstar result to this wave's non-AI markets is UNCERTAIN. **Verdict:** SURVIVES WITH CHANGES.
2. **It substitutes as well as enables.** Much measured gain is the same output made cheaper, which is excluded. The macro bound on that channel is modest: no more than about 0.66% TFP over 10 years as an upper bound, and less than 0.53% on the tighter estimate (Acemoglu, NBER w32487, May 2024; V1, claim 6, CONFIRMED). The paper is about the United States over roughly the next 10 years from 2024, and he calls the inputs speculative (V1).
3. **The layer is often supplied free by a platform**, so it is real for the producer but the rent stays with the platform, and the layer can be withdrawn.
4. **Long tails flood.** Titles multiply faster than consumption. Fully AI-generated music was 28% of Deezer's deliveries and about 0.5% of streams in September 2025, then 44% of daily uploads and about 1–3% of total streams by April 2026, mostly fraudulent and demonetised (03 §4, LEAD). Provenance, curation and certification become complements.
5. **The builders are entering the downstream layers themselves.** The opening is often where the incumbent refuses (01 §4 L12). That is context, not a find (§5.14).
6. **Barriers to entry are falling for reasons other than AI too.** Bernstein's project page says it went from mood board to finished sneaker in less than four months, and those pages do not mention AI (V1, claim 9, PARTLY). The brief's 500 pairs and under £50 a pair are not on the pages V1 fetched. Whether AI was used in the unreported work is UNCERTAIN (V1). Read the episode as evidence of the S1 shape in physical goods, not as evidence that AI did it.
7. **Regulation has a timetable.** Annex III high-risk obligations apply from 2 Dec 2027. High-risk systems embedded in products apply from 2 Aug 2028. Those dates were set by the AI Omnibus, in force since 27 Jul 2026 (European Commission page, fetched 16 Oct 2026; V1, claim 7, CONFIRMED). The high-risk list includes credit scoring, employment and education. It also includes biometrics, critical infrastructure, migration, asylum and border control (same page). That list hits **S3, S4 and one-person firms.** A chain can be price-ready and still blocked by an institution, or still queued for a wire (§3.2). **UNCERTAIN which transparency duties are in force on 16 Oct 2026.** The same Commission page says the Act became applicable on 2 Aug 2026, and, in another sentence, that transparency rules will come into effect in August 2026 (RV_B 2.4). A chain whose window depends on a transparency duty stays MECHANISM, with no kill date, until that date is settled.

| Phase | Who captures |

We record **viability, capture and fundability** on three lines. A chain can be real enablement and still a poor equity claim on the producer. A real purchase can be unfinanceable. In that case the claim is the pool.

### 3.7a Finance

Finance is a mechanism. Cheap experiments shift early capital toward bets whose prospects are revealed quickly, and away from complex assets whose first trial is expensive and uninformative (NBER w24523, 2018; Journal of Financial Economics, 2018). Housing, clinics, titles and batch-of-one plants are the second kind. The institution to name is the one that makes small tickets fundable: a pool, a servicer, a rating, a true-sale opinion, a residual-value or warranty policy. Credit scoring that decides access to a loan sits on the EU high-risk timetable from 2 Dec 2027 (Commission page, fetched 16 Oct 2026).

## 5. What we have learned, encoded as rules

1. **The route decides what forms.** **R1** is a write-down **with** transfer of control. **R2** is a write-down **without** transfer. Both are write-downs (01 §4 L1). **R3** is vintage deflation: broad diffusion, and no durable cost advantage (01 §4 L1; 01 §6.0). **R4** is a statutory or contracted price, revocable. **R5** is unsold inventory or a by-product, and it can found claims with no crash. **R0** is securities-only, and nothing passes downstream. Only some routes found third claims (01 L1). **R3 now:** the route running is R3, it is not accelerating, flagship list prices are rising, and posted prices have not followed (01 §6.0, §6.1). Cheap use, if it arrives, arrives through older tiers, open weights, and free platform layers. GPU-hour prices sit above P\*: B200 at $7.83 against $4.75 on 13 Oct 2026 (01 §6.0). The batch tier is not R5 (01 §6.1).
2. **No single lag is a screen** (01 §4 L11). Fibre's 1–5 years are not the 80-year or the 40-year productivity lags (§3.1).
3. **Value goes to the scarce complement.** Operators of the cheap asset earn utility returns (01 §4 L5). **For cognition, the analogue is printing, not fibre** (01 §4 L13). Holders of secrets lose. Holders of a seat, a cargo, a brand, or a record gain. Name the scarcity the industry rebuilds.
4. **The substrate transfers and the overlay is replaced.** In AI, the durable residue is energised capacity and interconnection, not chip generations (01 §4 L6).
5. **Leftover capacity in the builders' own market consolidates it. It does not found new industries** (01 §4 L7). A DRAM glut is pre-registered as restoration unless a named product pays only below the trough (01 §4 L15; 03 §6). The power-overbuild chain states its route, its substrate, the demand that would survive, and why it is not H01 or H09 (§3.5).
6. **Count consumption, revenue and revenue per title, not titles** (01 §4 L14). **Web fiction did not pass.** Do not treat a revenue share as a pass. WebNovel's AI-translated works were 40% of novel revenue in H1 2026 (issuer filing, unaudited interim, 11 Aug 2026) and revenue per AI title fell about 70% in 2025 (CALC). That is issuer narrative. No WebNovel revenue line exists. Independent revenue per title is absent. China Literature's relevant lines were flat or down (03 §1, §4, §10). Audiobooks failed: AI-narrated titles were 0.03% of reporting publishers' 2025 receipts (APA, Jun 2026). The self-published tail is unmeasured. A content tail is never the delivered list.
7. **A statute or public rail often *is* the cost fall.** Separate it from AI before crediting AI (01 §4 L16). If the statute or the public payment sets the price, the route is R4, or R4 plus AI. Historic R4 is kept and is not called AI. **R4 includes administrative capacity,** not only a new statute or a cheque. Where the second unlock is a permit, a title, a customs release, or a clinic licence, name the office, the current cycle time, and the cycle time at which the private output pays. The customer is the household or the firm buying the house, the deed, or the visit. A government that uses a model to draft its own papers is out, unless a named private party is thereby made viable. Internal chores are already common in OECD governments. Policymaking is 13 of 36 countries (36%). Accountability uses are 12 of 36 (33%). Most of these governments do not measure whether the pilot did anything (OECD, 15 Jun 2026). RV_B names Korea for a procurement assistant (about 70% less document-preparation time, and 99.9% compliance) and Brazil for an audit cycle (400 days to 8 days), both from that report. The quoted sentences do not themselves name the country. The named private party: no instance yet. A chain that depends on the pilot scaling carries that as the attack. **Verdict on this extension:** SURVIVES WITH CHANGES. Uneven uptake is measured. The translation into housing or titles is a dated forecast.
8. **After the AI cost falls, another constraint binds.** Credit, liquidity, liability, licence, consent, customs, attention, staffing, reorganization, critical mass, or a standard. Name it.
9. **A new business or a new market, not the old firm's old job done cheaper.** A service that reaches buyers who could not pay before is in (§4, gate 2). S13 passes the gates only as a new purchase (§3.5).
10. **Crowding (L15).** During scarcity the anchor buyer un-enables other products. That register is first-class (§3.8).
11. **Memorable stories fail when checked.** 20 corrections to the starting synthesis, among them the canal story (01 §3). No anecdote becomes a mechanism until its series, route and denominator have been checked.

**Further rules, one line each. Do not re-explain what 01 already explains.**

12. **L3.** Cheapness matters only when the input is a large share of unit cost, use is continuous or bulk, and value per unit is low. A small line item is diffusion (01 §4 L3). This is a grade (§4), not a gate.
13. **Denominator.** Every figure shows numerator, denominator, date, and whether it is a stock, a flow, a forecast, or a CALC. Score source integrity and claim status separately. Only a supported claim enters the synthesis (RV_A 3.10, from the M1 lessons; applied here).
14. **J1–J6, readings of 14 Oct 2026, not the historic definitions alone** (01 §6.4). J1 EARLY, acceleration FAILS. J2 NOT FIRING. J3 NOT FIRING, precursors only. J4 EARLY, and it is adoption, not a new business. J5 NOT FIRING. J6 FIRING, only inside the excluded set. J4 or an incumbent refusal is not the find.
15. **Argon guard.** 1 of 28 cases fully supported, 16 of 28 direct users, 8 of 28 inside or beside the supply chain (04). A page is not a result.
16. **Quality step ≠ cost collapse.** eBay 2014 is a completed pre-build-out case, +10.9%, on a layer that was already free (03 §1, §3).
17. **Platform access binds** before the model price. Google Play auto-narration was free by 2022 and left no measured market (03). Publishers saved 20% then 50%, against promises of 85–95% (03 §3).
18. **Perishability.** The scarce piece is the cold chain or the time-critical network, not the cheap haul (01 §4 L4).
19. **Untested, named, not an empty drawer** (01 §6.6 and §7). AI-built fibre. Skills beyond one anecdote. Materials-science modelling. Veterinary and insurance-broker roll-ups. Mid-size insurers and distributors. H03 mail order was not classified because sources timed out. Cloudflare has no cost evidence yet.
20. **Three outputs every time.** Direct use is excluded. Diffusion is reported as who captures now. The find is new output plus the ecosystem (RV_A 3.15).

## 6. Method: the chain register

Each chain is a record with these fields. Cost, route, threshold, window, enabled party, conjunction, and the downstream ecosystem must be filled. **Open** is allowed only on a trigger reading or a review date.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Cost and route | what gets cheaper, by which route (R1–R5), on which clock |
| Unit and trajectory | the unit, today's level, trend, and the sellable-quality price. Numerator, denominator, date, and stock, flow, forecast, or CALC |
| Threshold | the cost level at which the enabled output pays (CALC, shown) |
| Window | the crossing window, and the complement that sets it, named and dated |
| Enabled party | named; the customer buys something non-AI; new output, not substitution |
| Conjunction | the second unlock it needs, and its own trigger |
| Ecosystem | at least the scarce complement and the second-order trades (layers 3 and 9 of §3.4), filled, or the chain does not count toward the 15. The full nine-layer map is kept for five chains (§7.2) |
| Capture | who takes the rent; whether the layer can be withdrawn. A separate line for fundability (§3.7) |
| Next constraint | what binds after the crossing |
| Attack | the strongest case against, and a verdict |
| Triggers | one primary trigger, plus the others; today's reading and the confirming reading. **Open** allowed on a reading |
| Status | MECHANISM → EMERGING → EVIDENCED, or KILLED; with a review date. "Not yet" is recorded as MECHANISM plus its window date. **Open** allowed on the date |

### 6a. The blocker register (the project owner, 16 Oct 2026: "what binds now" is the most important thing to collect)

- **Every chain names its margin and its blocker.** The margin is the entrants or buyers out today, with a count, or UNCOUNTED with a fill plan (11 §6.3). The blocker is the cost or constraint that keeps them out.
- **Each blocker is quantified, or unpriced with a fill plan:**
  - its cost per marginal unit;
  - its share of the marginal unit's cost;
  - the size of the margin it blocks;
  - why it blocks: a fixed cost per variety, a per-site presence, a statute, liability, density or physical work;
  - whether it is cognitive;
  - what removed it elsewhere;
  - AI's measured cut today, the cut needed, and the distance.
- **Blockers are classified by mechanism, not by industry.** The classes live in `08_Blocker_Register.md`.
- **The cross-map is the extrapolation engine.** For each class it lists every industry where the class binds, observed or hypothesised. A blocker that moves in one industry triggers a scan of every other industry in its class. AI's measured cut on a class is read once and applied across it.
- **The observation log.** Each blocker carries four lines: what we know, what we predicted (locked and dated), what we observed, and what worked (what removed it, AI or not).

**Registers that cannot delete each other:**

- **mechanism**, which includes chains that do not yet pay;
- **evidence**, which holds only re-read, correctly denominated facts;
- **capture**, and **diffusion** on its own line (who is winning now), so diffusion is not dropped;
- **untested**, and the classes in §5.19 are named in it.

**The rules of evidence:**

- Verification corrects a number, a date, a denominator or a placement. It never changes the question or deletes a class.
- **Not yet** is a valid status only inside a window dated by route (§3.1). No window is longer than that unless the complement that sets it is named and dated.
- A chain is **killed** when that window passes and the constraint still binds, or when the receipt is an AI product. It is not killed because the cost share is small. That is a grade (§4).
- Killed rows stay on the register. They are not re-read.
- **Pre-register.** Tests, routes and conditional claims are written before the parcels return. A changed rule is the project owner's decision, dated, and applied going forward.
- **Forecasts are append-only.** A new reading may add a new dated line. It never edits an old one (§7).
- **The register has one writer.** After the project owner adjudicates, Claude appends the line to the register and its change log. Each untested class in §5.19 gets a review date or stays outside the 15.
