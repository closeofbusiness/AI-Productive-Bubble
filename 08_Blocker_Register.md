## External read: McKinsey/QuantumBlack, "The AI value curve" (Oct 2026). Grok VC1, no web; Claude verified 4 quotes verbatim

**Verdict: marginally useful.** It is about applying AI to existing work, which is diffusion under gate 2. Its $2.52 trillion is a sum of incumbent hours multiplied by a modest average gap: 38% of it is the US, and 64% sits in four sectors. **Do not cite it as AI's cut on any blocker.** Five items are kept, as caveats or hypotheses; none is a price series.

1. **The full-cost identity.**
   - AI's cut equals the fully loaded labour cost, minus tokens, minus human review, validation and rework. The article publishes no split.
   - **Rule for this register:** an API list price on the panel is only an *upper bound* on AI's cut until the review and rework minutes are known.
   - Test: Tepper's ratio. An agent pays only if its probability of success exceeds the time to verify divided by the time for a human to do the work.
2. **The sign flips with the local wage.** AI prices are global and wages are local. Office and admin work is modelled positive under US costs, but "may not yet be in markets where labor costs fall below the effective cost of AI".
   - **Implication for classes 10 and 13** (suppliers in BR, MX, ID and TH): a use case that pays for a US buyer may not pay in the supplier's country. The cross-border wedge (buyer wage minus supplier wage) is not modelled. UNCERTAIN.
3. **Negative cuts, modelled.** For pharmacy technicians and entertainment attendants, AI costs more than prevailing wages. Seven physical archetypes, including construction, maintenance and agriculture, show limited gen-AI savings. **A low wage can make the cut negative before any statute, sample or presence binds.**
4. **The "full cost" stops at review.**
   - Inside the cost: review, validation and rework.
   - Outside it: statute, liability, workforce rules, latency, privacy and one-time reorganisation.
   - **That matches the register's stack:** quality, then trust, then latency, then privacy rules. A positive McKinsey cell does not mean those blockers have moved.
5. **A fragility check.** If AI's cost rises 30%, the global pool drops by 34% ($2.52 trillion → $1.67 trillion), and some cells flip negative (an illustration, not a forecast). **A crossing only slightly under the human cost is fragile.** Record the margin of the crossing, not just its sign.

## Round F, 25 Oct 2026: reuse hypotheses, trust, and the context classes (Grok T02, T03, S07–S14, with web, all clean; `_grok_returns/`)

### Test reuse in five new industries (T02, T03)

**The incumbency pattern holds** for fire-test extended application, ISO 15614 welding-procedure ranges, vehicle worst-case and revision rules, pressure-equipment type examination, and particular material appraisals. In each, the holder's next variant is spared, and a first geometry or procedure pays in full.

**Four rules break it, and they show what an entrant-enabling rule looks like:**
1. **REACH Art. 27: a priced right to refer.**
   - A later registrant must request vertebrate-test data, and may request other data.
   - If the holder refuses, the Agency can give the newcomer permission to refer once it has paid a proportionate share of the cost.
   - Claude verified the wording on legislation.gov.uk on 25 Oct.
   - The limit: same substance only, so a new substance is still outside.
2. **ISO 15612: adopting a published standard welding procedure.** Any fabricator may adopt a procedure another body has qualified and published; EN 1090-2 names the route at EXC2.
3. **CPR Art. 36(1)(a), and the PED Art. 12 harmonised materials and European approvals for materials.** A published classification or material qualification that any maker may use without the holder's consent.
4. **Approval marks that travel on the part** (lamps, tyres), and EU individual vehicle approval accepting another applicant's system report for the same characteristics.

**The rule for the register:**
- **The enabler is a file that is not held by one seller.** Three forms work:
  - (a) the law replaces the holder's veto with a priced right to refer;
  - (b) a standard publishes a procedure any qualified user may adopt;
  - (c) a public decision turns a body of tests into a class.
- **Where AI could ride on this:** drafting the adoption, mapping a first product onto a published class, or the read-across file.
- **But no rule found accepts a model in place of the physical test.**
- Add to the observation log: track REACH-style compulsory sharing, standard procedures, and published-class decisions as **class 3 unlocks**.

### The rule from round I (26 Oct 2026): when a design file is enablement and when it is diffusion

A fabrication-then-file chain is **enablement only if all three hold:**
1. **The file is bespoke**, not a template.
2. **The human file is dear relative to the order.** For example, a $75–200 file on a one-part bracket, or €5,500 on a €6,700 photonic seat.
3. **No non-AI tool already covers that margin** (templates, parametric generators, diagram editors, built-in proposals).

## Round OW (30 Oct 2026): testing the EU orphan-works lead

Question: in the EU, where the Orphan Works Directive 2012/28/EU is still in force, is the per-work diligent search the binding blocker? Grok OW01 (adversarial test of the directive) and OW02 (the other EU routes), both web-enabled. Returns: `_grok_returns/OW01_answer.md`, `OW02_answer.md`. Prompts: `_parcels/round_OW/`.

**Verdict: CONTEXT, not a candidate chain.** The search binds first only for a non-commercial margin that admits no business.
- **The Directive is in force and unused.**
  - 7,597 works were in the EUIPO database on 5 Oct 2022, against the Commission's illustrative stock of 125M–250M orphan print volumes: 0.003–0.006%. The numerator is MEASURED; the denominator is the 2011 impact assessment's 5–10% of 2.5 billion volumes, an ESTIMATE.
  - Before Brexit the database held 18,649 works (June 2020), 61% of them recorded by UK organisations.
  - A later reading of the portal, from a secondary source, gives 7,090 (Sept 2025).
- **For eligible institutions, the search is the main blocker they report.** The Commission's SWD(2022) 412 calls it "the main reason for its ineffectiveness" (Claude verified the quote). EUIPO's 2017 survey:
  - the search takes 2–6 hours per work (self-reported);
  - 43.3% of beneficiaries rated it "very important", against 37.3% for funding;
  - national lists run from 25 sources (Finland) to 229 (Germany);
  - EnDOW counted more than 1,700 sources, 37% of them not free.
- **A statute binds above the search for everyone else.** Arts 1, 2 and 6 limit the exception to libraries, archives, museums, educational establishments, film and audio heritage bodies and public-service broadcasters, for public-interest, non-commercial use. Revenue may cover only the cost of digitising.
  - Recital 22 bars a commercial partner from any right to use the works.
  - Stand-alone photographs are excluded, and so are broadcaster productions after 2002.
  - A rightholder the search does find must still consent (Art 2(2)).
  - Compensation is owed if an owner reappears (Art 6(5)).
  - The Commission rejected widening the exception to publishers (15% support).
- **Gate 2:** a near-free search admits no commercial entrant, publisher, individual or AI-training use. On the institutional margin it would widen access to collections, but digitisation funding, the separate search for each embedded work, and the compensation risk remain. This margin is a public good, not a business.
